Warm paper, not a dashboard

Revised on your three notes: matching words stay side by side, nothing needs new Slovak or Ukrainian copy, and the whole thing moves onto warm paper with soft cards and a bit of tactility instead of enterprise chrome.

v2.0.0-alpha.1 Revision 2 — supersedes rev 1 11 Sep 2026 Proposal — nothing implemented yet

What changed in this revision

Your three notes, and what each one actually forced

1 · Words stay side by side

My mistake in rev 1 — I stacked them into two groups, which quietly broke the exercise. WordsExerciseView is a two-column matching game: LeftColumn holds the Ukrainian keys, RightColumn the Slovak values, and you tap one on each side to pair them. Stacking them removes the spatial relationship the whole interaction depends on. The two-column layout is preserved below and everything else was designed around it.

2 · No new copy — this turned out to help

Every mockup below uses only strings that already exist in translations/sk.ts and ua.ts, or content that already comes from Firestore. There is a full string-by-string inventory further down so you can check it.

This constraint improved the design rather than limiting it. The most dashboard-ish things in rev 1 — the 142 SLOV / 3 MODULY / 68% HOTOVO stat row, the POKRAČUJ and VŠETKY MODULY section labels, the "Dokonči Modul 2 na odomknutie" locked message — all needed new copy, and all of them were exactly what made it feel corporate. Removing them left hierarchy to be carried by layout, weight and colour, which is what you actually wanted.

3 · Warm paper, soft cards, a little tactility

You're right about Codex's background — a warm cream reads calm and paper-like in a way a cool near-white can't. I've taken that and gone further from the dashboard: no visible card borders (soft shadow instead), radii up from 10px to 20–22px, progress as segment pips rather than percentage bars, and a 3px bottom edge on buttons and active pills so they feel like physical keys. That last one is the cheapest "game" signal there is.

What I did not take from Codex's version: the extra explanatory copy (rule 2), the legend line at the bottom, and the density. Yours is a drill app used in short bursts — it wants fewer things on screen, larger.

Why pips

Pips vs. bar vs. percentage

A percentage is a report. Pips are a board — you can see how many steps are left and count them, which is what makes progress feel like a game rather than a metric.

They also need no copy, work at any width, and read correctly at a glance in both themes. And they degrade well: a 40-item lesson renders as a thin segmented line rather than 40 unreadable dots, because each pip is flex:1.

The existing ProgressBar component already receives exactly what's needed — status and goal — so this is a swap of its internals, not a data change.

Type and texture

Where the "notepad, not dashboard" feeling actually comes from

Nunito for headings, Inter for body

Rev 1 recommended Inter throughout. Inter is excellent and neutral — which is precisely the problem for the feeling you're after. Nunito has rounded terminals and a warmer, softer skeleton, covers Latin Extended and Cyrillic, and reads friendly without tipping into childish. Using it for display and headings only, with Inter for body copy, keeps long Slovak sentences crisp while making every heading and number feel handmade.

Nunito-only is a fine simpler option — it holds up at 16px body size. Inter-only is the safest and least distinctive.

The four texture moves

1 · Soft shadow instead of borders

Cards get e2 and no border at all. The paper-to-card step is deliberately subtle (1.07:1) — the shadow does the lifting. Hairline borders on every box is the single most dashboard-ish habit in the current UI.

2 · Radii up to 20–22px

From a uniform 10px. Large radii on warm paper read as soft physical objects; 10px reads as a 2016 web panel. Pills and buttons stay fully round.

3 · A 3px bottom edge on anything pressable

Buttons and active pills get box-shadow: 0 3px 0 <darker step>. Combined with a spring that removes it on press, the control feels like a key going down. This is the cheapest and most effective "game" cue available, and it costs one line per component.

4 · Pips instead of bars and percentages

Countable steps rather than a measured quantity. No copy, no numbers, and it turns the Unit screen from a status report into a board.

Sequence

Six phases, accessibility first, no big-bang rewrite

1Warm tokens + the six contrast fixes~half a day
  • Add themes/tokens.ts with the ramps; map both themes onto semantic names.
  • Keep the old flat colors keys as deprecated aliases so nothing breaks mid-migration.
  • Swap the paper/night neutrals in — this alone is most of the visual change.
  • Fix the active ua pill first. One line, 1.46:1 → 8.02:1.
2Nunito + type scale~2 hours
  • Load Nunito and Inter alongside the existing Octicons.font call.
  • Add a Text atom with a variant prop; migrate per screen.
3Buttons, pills, the bottom edge~half a day
  • Button variants: primary / success / danger / secondary.
  • Unify the four pill components onto one base taking state and language.
  • Add the 3px bottom edge and the press spring together — they only work as a pair.
4Cards and pips~1 day
  • Merge MainCard and OutlinedCard into one Card with variants; drop borders for e2.
  • Rebuild ProgressBar internals as pips — same status/goal props.
  • Delete the 80px HomeStats padding; move Pokračovať onto the active module card.
  • Real locked state instead of opacity: 0.6.
5Motion and haptics~half a day
  • reanimated and expo-haptics are installed with zero imports — press springs, pip fills, a haptic on match.
  • One usePressScale hook and one feedback.ts helper covers all of it.
6Night pass + audit~half a day
  • Verify the warm-charcoal surfaces on device; re-run contrast on every pair.
  • Add a token snapshot test so ratios can't regress; delete the phase-1 aliases.

Still your call

1 · How warm is too warm?

#F7F4EC is a moderate cream — noticeably paper, still calm. Codex's looks a touch warmer. It's a one-value change either way, so worth eyeballing on your own screen before phase 1.

2 · Nunito, or stay neutral?

Nunito is what makes it feel handmade rather than generic, and it covers both scripts. If you'd rather not commit to personality yet, Inter-only still gets you most of the way — the warm paper and soft cards are doing more work than the typeface.

3 · Green for correct, still?

Unchanged from rev 1 and still my recommendation. The current sage #A1CEC1 is already off-flag, so this isn't adding a hue so much as fixing one — and it takes the worst contrast failure in the app off the board.

4 · Amber beyond the ua pill — v2.1?

You mentioned hints as a future use. Nothing in this revision adds them, since they'd need copy. Worth noting that the ramp is sized for it already: amber-50 surface with amber-900 text is 10.24:1, so a hint block can drop in later with no palette work.
